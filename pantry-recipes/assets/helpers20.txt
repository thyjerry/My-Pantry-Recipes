# @ problem | kind of dish ; - fix ; ! prevent next time
@ Too salty | soups, stews, sauces
- Add more of everything except salt: unsalted stock, water, tomatoes, beans or cooked rice, then simmer to taste.
- Stir in something rich or creamy (unsalted butter, cream, coconut milk, yogurt off the heat). Fat softens how salty it tastes.
- A squeeze of lemon or a little vinegar can mask mild saltiness.
- Serve over plain rice, pasta, potatoes or bread with no salt added.
- A raw potato does not pull salt out. Diluting is what actually works.
! Season gradually and taste near the end, because liquids get saltier as they reduce.
@ Too spicy | curries, chilli, sauces
- Add dairy or coconut: yogurt, sour cream, cream, coconut milk or grated cheese.
- Make a bigger batch with no chilli in it (more beans, tomatoes, stock or meat) and mix the two.
- A little sugar or honey and a squeeze of lime help balance the heat.
- Serve with cooling sides: plain rice, bread, cucumber, yogurt.
! Add chilli in small amounts and taste. Seeds and membranes hold much of the heat.
@ Too sour or acidic | tomato sauces, dressings, soups
- Add a pinch of sugar or a little honey, then taste.
- Stir in butter, cream or olive oil to round it out.
- For tomato sauce, a tiny pinch of baking soda (1/8 tsp per 4 cups) neutralises some acid. It will foam, so stir and taste.
- Make more of the dish without the acidic ingredient and combine.
! Add lemon juice and vinegar at the end, a little at a time.
@ Too sweet | sauces, curries, glazes
- Add acid: lemon or lime juice, or vinegar.
- Add salt, or something salty like soy sauce.
- Add heat or bitterness: chilli, black pepper, mustard or cocoa (in savoury chilli).
- Make more of the dish without the sweet part and mix.
! Sweet ingredients concentrate as they reduce. Add sugar last.
@ Bitter | greens, coffee-based sauces, burnt garlic
- Add a little salt first, because salt reduces bitterness.
- Balance with sweetness (honey, sugar, sweet vegetables) and fat (butter, cream).
- If garlic or spices scorched, start the base again. Burnt garlic does not cook out.
! Cook garlic gently for under a minute. Blanch bitter greens before sautéing.
@ Bland or flat | anything savoury
- Add salt a pinch at a time and taste after each pinch.
- Add acid: lemon, lime or vinegar. This is often what is missing.
- Add savoury depth: soy sauce, fish sauce, tomato paste, parmesan or a stock cube.
- Finish with fresh herbs, black pepper or a drizzle of good oil.
! Season each stage (onions, meat, liquid) instead of only at the end.
@ Sauce too thin | gravies, stews, pan sauces
- Simmer uncovered to reduce.
- Whisk 1 tbsp cornstarch into 2 tbsp cold water, stir it in and simmer 1 to 2 minutes.
- Mash some of the beans or potatoes into the liquid.
- Mix equal parts soft butter and flour into a paste and whisk in small pieces.
! Leave the lid off for the last part of cooking.
@ Sauce too thick | gravies, cheese sauces, stews
- Whisk in warm stock, milk or water a splash at a time.
- Taste again after thinning and re-season.
! Sauces thicken as they stand. Take them off the heat a little thinner than you want.
@ Sauce split or curdled | cream sauces, custards, mayonnaise
- Take it off the heat at once.
- Cream or cheese sauce: whisk in a spoonful of cold cream, or blend briefly.
- Mayonnaise or hollandaise: start a new yolk (or 1 tbsp warm water) in a clean bowl and slowly whisk the split sauce into it.
- Custard: strain, then blend. It will be thinner but smooth.
! Use gentle heat, add acid off the heat, and warm dairy before it goes into hot liquid.
@ Lumpy sauce or gravy | white sauce, gravy
- Whisk hard off the heat.
- Push it through a sieve, or use a stick blender.
! Add liquid gradually to the roux and whisk the whole time.
@ Burnt on the bottom | stews, rice, beans
- Do not stir. Pour the unburnt part into a clean pot straight away.
- Taste it. If it tastes smoky, add a little dairy, acid or sugar and serve with strong sides.
! Use a heavy pot, lower heat and stir from the bottom. Add liquid when it looks dry.
@ Overcooked pasta or rice | pasta, rice
- Pasta: rinse in cold water and pan-fry, or bake it with sauce and cheese.
- Mushy rice: spread on a tray to steam dry, then use for fried rice, rice pudding or rice cakes.
! Start tasting pasta 2 minutes before the packet time.
@ Undercooked rice | rice
- Add 2 to 4 tbsp hot water, cover tightly and steam on very low heat for 5 to 10 minutes.
- Or cover and put in a low oven for 10 minutes.
! Keep the lid on and let it rest 10 minutes after cooking.
@ Dry or tough meat | roasts, chops, chicken breast
- Slice thinly against the grain.
- Serve with a sauce, gravy or the pan juices.
- Shred it and simmer briefly in stock or sauce (tacos, sandwiches, soup).
- Tough stewing cuts usually need more time, not less. Keep simmering until tender.
! Use a thermometer and rest meat before cutting.
@ Rubbery eggs | scrambled eggs, omelettes
- Fold in a spoonful of cream, butter or soft cheese off the heat.
! Cook on low heat and take them off while still slightly wet.
@ Soggy fried or roasted food | fries, roast vegetables, breaded food
- Re-crisp on a rack in a hot oven (220 C / 425 F) for 5 to 10 minutes.
! Dry food well, do not crowd the pan and get the oil hot first.
@ Cake sank or is dense | cakes, quick breads
- Cut out the sunken middle and fill it with fruit and cream, or make trifle.
- Crumble a dense cake into crumbs for a crumble topping or cake pops.
! Check that baking powder is fresh, measure flour by weight and do not open the oven early.
@ Cookies spread too much | cookies
- Trim warm cookies into neat circles with a round cutter.
! Chill the dough for 30 minutes, use a cool tray and check the butter was not melted.
@ Dough will not rise | bread, pizza dough
- Move it somewhere warmer, such as an oven with only the light on.
- If the yeast was dead, knead in a fresh pinch of yeast dissolved in a little warm water.
- Flat dough still makes flatbread or crackers.
! Water should feel warm, not hot. Check the yeast date.
@ Too greasy | stews, soups, sauces
- Chill and lift off the set fat, or skim with a spoon.
- Lay a paper towel briefly on the surface to soak up fat.
! Brown meat first and pour off extra fat before adding liquid.
@ Too much garlic or onion | sauces, dressings
- Add more of the other ingredients to dilute it.
- Simmer longer, because cooked garlic is milder than raw.
- Add acid and a little sweetness to balance.
! Remember that raw garlic gets stronger as a dressing sits.
@ Seized chocolate | melted chocolate
- Stir in 1 teaspoon of hot water or neutral oil at a time until smooth. It works for sauces and drizzles, not tempering.
! Keep all water and steam out of melting chocolate.
@ Whipped cream over-whipped | whipped cream
- Gently fold in a few spoonfuls of liquid cream by hand.
- If it is grainy, keep whipping to make butter.
! Stop when soft peaks form, and finish by hand.
@ Soup too thick or stodgy | soups
- Thin with hot stock or water, then re-season.
! Starchy soups (lentils, potatoes) thicken as they cool.
@ Pastry cracked or torn | pie crust, tart shells
- Patch with leftover pastry, using a little water as glue.
- For a blind-baked shell, brush cracks with beaten egg and bake 2 minutes to seal.
! Let chilled pastry soften slightly before rolling.
