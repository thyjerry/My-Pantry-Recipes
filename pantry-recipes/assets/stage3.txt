[fasting]
# @ id | label | rules (veg, vegan, nomeat, noegg, noallium, nograin, nolegume, nohoney, noroot)
# n: note shown with the label
@ meatless | Meatless day (Lent, Catholic) | nomeat
n: No meat from land animals or poultry. Fish, seafood, eggs and dairy are fine. Kept on Ash Wednesday and the Fridays of Lent, and by some on every Friday.
@ orthodox | Orthodox fast (strict days) | vegan
n: No meat, fish, dairy or eggs. On many days olive oil and wine are also left out, and shellfish is often allowed. Ethiopian and Eritrean fasting (tsom) has similar rules.
@ vrat | Vrat (Navratri and other Hindu fasts) | veg, noegg, noallium, nograin, nolegume
n: No grains, pulses, onion, garlic, meat or eggs. Cooks use buckwheat (kuttu), water chestnut flour (singhara), amaranth, sago (sabudana), potatoes, nuts, dairy and rock salt (sendha namak). Customs vary by family and region.
@ ekadashi | Ekadashi | veg, noegg, nograin, nolegume
n: No grains or beans on the eleventh day of each lunar fortnight. Many also avoid onion and garlic.
@ jain | Jain-friendly | veg, noegg, nohoney, noroot, noallium
n: No meat, eggs or honey, and no root vegetables (onion, garlic, potato, carrot, beetroot, radish, fresh ginger and similar). Many also avoid eating after sunset. Dried ginger and turmeric are usually fine.
@ buddhist | Buddhist vegetarian (no pungent vegetables) | veg, noallium
n: No meat or fish, and none of the five pungent vegetables (onion, garlic, leek, chives and scallion). Some traditions also leave out eggs and dairy.

[bake-trouble]
# @ problem | bakes ; - likely cause: fix
@ Bread is dense and heavy | bread
- Under-proofed: let it rise until roughly doubled and a floured finger dent springs back slowly.
- Too much flour: weigh flour, or spoon it into the cup and level it instead of scooping.
- Yeast was old or the water too hot: test yeast in warm water (about 38 °C / 100 °F) with a pinch of sugar; it should foam in 10 minutes.
@ Bread collapsed or spread flat | bread
- Over-proofed: shape it again, give it a shorter second rise and bake as soon as the dent springs back slowly.
- Dough too wet for a free-form loaf: use a tin, or hold back 5-10% of the water next time.
- Weak shaping: pull the surface tight so the loaf holds its shape.
@ Crust too pale or too soft | bread, pastry
- Oven not hot enough: preheat for at least 20 minutes and check with an oven thermometer.
- Add steam in the first 10 minutes (a tray of hot water), then let it bake dry.
- Brush with egg wash for a deeper colour.
@ Cake sank in the middle | cake
- Oven door opened too early: wait until at least two thirds of the baking time has passed.
- Too much raising agent or sugar: measure level spoons and check altitude adjustments.
- Underbaked: a skewer in the centre should come out clean or with a few dry crumbs.
@ Cake domed and cracked | cake
- Oven too hot: lower it by 10-15 °C and bake a little longer.
- Batter over-mixed after adding flour: fold until just combined.
- Use bake-even strips or a damp towel strip around the tin.
@ Cake dry or crumbly | cake
- Overbaked: start checking 5-10 minutes before the time given.
- Too much flour: weigh it.
- Brush layers with a little sugar syrup to rescue a dry cake.
@ Cookies spread too much | cookies
- Butter too soft or melted: chill the dough for 30 minutes before baking.
- Tray was warm: let trays cool between batches.
- Too little flour or too much sugar: weigh both.
@ Cookies did not spread and stayed puffy | cookies
- Too much flour: weigh it, or press the balls down a little before baking.
- Dough too cold: let chilled dough warm 10 minutes.
@ Soggy pastry base | pastry, pie
- Bake the case blind first (lined with paper and baking beans), then without beans until dry.
- Bake on a preheated tray on the lowest rack.
- Brush the baked case with beaten egg and return it for 2 minutes to seal it.
@ Pastry shrank in the tin | pastry, pie
- Overworked dough: handle lightly and rest it in the fridge for 30 minutes before rolling and again before baking.
- Do not stretch the pastry into the tin; ease it in and leave an overhang to trim after baking.
@ Tough pastry | pastry, pie
- Too much water or kneading: add water a spoon at a time and stop as soon as it holds together.
- Keep fat and water cold.
@ Muffins tunnelled or peaked | muffins
- Over-mixed batter: stir until just combined; a few lumps are fine.
- Oven too hot: check with a thermometer.
@ Brownies too cakey | brownies
- Too much flour or egg, or too much beating: fold gently and bake only until a skewer shows moist crumbs.
@ Sourdough starter not rising | sourdough
- Too cold: keep it at 24-27 °C (75-80 °F).
- Feed regularly at the same times and use some wholegrain flour.
- A new starter can take 7-14 days to become reliable.
@ Sourdough too sour | sourdough
- Use a younger starter (feed it 4-6 hours before mixing) and a shorter, warmer rise.
@ Meringue weeping or sticky | meringue
- Sugar not dissolved: add it slowly and beat until the mix feels smooth between your fingers.
- Humid day: bake low and leave the meringues to cool in the switched-off oven.
@ Custard or cheesecake cracked | cheesecake, custard
- Overbaked or too hot: bake until the centre still wobbles, then cool slowly in the oven with the door ajar.
- A water bath keeps the heat gentle.
@ Bottom burned, top pale | all
- Rack too low or a dark tin: move up one rack, or use a light-coloured tin or a second tray underneath.
@ Fruit sank to the bottom | cake, muffins
- Toss the fruit in a spoon of the measured flour and use a slightly thicker batter.
@ Scones did not rise | scones
- Old baking powder: replace it every 6-12 months.
- Cut straight down without twisting, and keep the dough thick (2.5 cm / 1 in).
